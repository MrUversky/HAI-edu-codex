# Signal Map

## Main signal clusters

### 1. Participants need transfer, not spectacle
Evidence:
- repeated requests for practical relevance
- warnings against abstract examples
- desire to leave with something usable next week

### 2. The team wants to teach agentic process, not prompt folklore
Evidence:
- repeated focus on workflow, agents, and structure
- explicit rejection of "100 prompts for every situation"

### 3. The audience is mixed and fragile to overload
Evidence:
- mixed maturity
- fear of too many tools
- concern that GitHub/Codex may be too complex for some participants

### 4. The session must create commercial and methodological continuation
Evidence:
- desire for bridge to deeper workshop / pilot / program
- sales interest in clarifying next steps for companies

## Key tensions
- **wow vs transfer**: the experience needs energy, but must end in applicability
- **freedom vs scaffolding**: too much freedom creates chaos; too much structure blocks ownership
- **modern tooling vs accessibility**: Codex/GitHub can support realism, but may overload beginners
- **general case vs personal case**: shared case is necessary first; personal application comes later

## Likely bottlenecks
1. **Overloading the participant too early**
   If the lesson jumps into personal use cases or complex interfaces before people understand the mechanics, confusion rises fast.

2. **Mistaking demo quality for lesson quality**
   A strong demo alone will not create transfer unless the mechanics are unpacked and touched by participants.

3. **Trying to solve too much in one session**
   The lesson can easily become a mix of AI overview, Codex tutorial, strategy session, and workshop design. That would dilute the core value.

## Why it matters
If these bottlenecks are not handled, participants will leave with one of two bad outcomes:
- "that was cool, but I don't know what to do with it"
- "I got lost in tools before I understood the point"

## Recommended design focus
The lesson should optimize for this path:
1. observe a shared agent workflow
2. understand the architecture
3. touch the mechanism
4. only then map it to one personal next step
